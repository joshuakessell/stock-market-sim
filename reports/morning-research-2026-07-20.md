### Medical

#### Dyne Therapeutics (DYN, Nasdaq) — Impact: High
**What happened:** On July 20, 2026, Dyne announced the FDA accepted its BLA for z-rostudirsen (DYNE-251) for Duchenne muscular dystrophy amenable to exon 51 skipping, with Priority Review and a PDUFA target action date of January 21, 2027. Filed as 8-K Item 8.01. [SEC EDGAR 8-K, CIK 1818794](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1818794/000119312526308343/dyn-20260720.htm)
**Market impact:** DYN traded roughly flat this morning ($23.75 vs. $23.74 prior close) — a muted reaction to what's normally a de-risking event, suggesting the market had largely priced in acceptance.
**Recommendation: Buy** — BLA acceptance with Priority Review is a real, dated regulatory milestone that narrows the path to a 2027 launch, though it doesn't guarantee approval and the FDA could still issue a CRL at action. The flat reaction tempers near-term upside expectations. Monitor the PDUFA date (Jan 21, 2027) and any advisory-committee scheduling.

#### Kalaris Therapeutics (KLRS, Nasdaq) — Impact: Medium
**What happened:** On July 17, 2026, Kalaris announced expanded Phase 1a single-ascending-dose data for TH103 in treatment-naive nAMD patients (17 treatment-naive + 3 treatment-experienced, all completing 6-month follow-up), reporting 27–53x lower Cmax vs. leading anti-VEGF agents and 41% of patients not needing retreatment before 4 months. [SEC EDGAR 8-K, CIK 1754068](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1754068/000119312526306737/d125006d8k.htm)
**Market impact:** Despite the positive framing, KLRS is down roughly 5% since the announcement ($4.155 vs. $4.38 prior close) — a real conflict between the press-release tone and the market reaction worth flagging.
**Recommendation: Watch** — Still early-stage (Phase 1a, N=20) durability/PK data, not efficacy proof in a controlled setting, and the market's negative reaction suggests skepticism about differentiation versus existing anti-VEGF agents. Monitor for Phase 2 initiation details and whether the retreatment-interval advantage holds in a larger, randomized cohort.

### Deals

#### Jasper Therapeutics (JSPR, Nasdaq) — Impact: High [Deal]
**What happened:** On July 16, 2026 (filed July 17), Jasper completed an all-stock reverse merger with private Kira Pharmaceuticals plus a concurrent $132M PIPE. Kira equityholders end up with about 49.9% of the combined company post-financing. The combined pipeline includes KP-104 (bifunctional complement inhibitor, Phase 2/3-ready) and briquilimab (anti-KIT, pre-BLA stage), with a CVR entitling legacy Jasper holders to up to $30M tied to a briquilimab Priority Review Voucher by end-2028. [SEC EDGAR 8-K, CIK 1788028](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1788028/000121390026078887/ea0298229-8k_jasper.htm)
**Market impact:** JSPR fell roughly 11% since the deal closed ($0.791 vs. $0.885 prior close) — a sharp negative reaction typical of shell-merger dilution.
**Recommendation: Watch** — Creates a materially larger, better-funded immunology company, but the deal is complex (preferred-stock conversion still requires a stockholder vote within 120 days), heavily diluted, and the market's initial reaction signals real skepticism. Monitor the stockholder vote outcome and Q4 2026 KP-104 Stage 1 interim data.

#### Tempus AI (TEM, Nasdaq) / Personalis (PSNL, Nasdaq) — Impact: High [Deal]
**What happened:** On July 20, 2026, Tempus AI announced a definitive agreement to acquire Personalis at a nominal $16.25/share (6% premium to Friday's close, 28% to unaffected 30-day VWAP), structured as 100% stock via a floating exchange ratio capped at 0.3356 TEM shares per PSNL share, with Tempus able to elect up to 50% cash at its discretion. Deal value about $1.5B; closing expected late 2026/early 2027, subject to PSNL shareholder approval and regulatory clearance. [Personalis 8-K, CIK 1527753](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1527753/000119312526308488/d68794dex991.htm); [Tempus 8-K, CIK 1717115](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1717115/000119312526308469/d124162d8k.htm)
**Market impact:** Notable conflict between headline and tape: TEM fell about 8.9% ($47.82 vs. $52.47) and PSNL fell about 12% ($13.54 vs. $15.39) — both well below the announced $16.25 nominal value. Because the exchange ratio floats and is capped rather than fixed-dollar, TEM's own decline mechanically drags down the deal's realized value for PSNL holders.
**Recommendation: Watch** (both legs) — For TEM: real strategic logic bolting MRD capability onto an AI oncology platform, but dilution and integration risk, and the market's initial reaction was negative. For PSNL: this is effectively merger arbitrage now — the discount to headline value reflects real closing risk and the floating-ratio mechanic, not a simple mispricing. Monitor the shareholder vote date and any regulatory review timeline once disclosed.
